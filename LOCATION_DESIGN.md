# Systemic location design

Locations are social/ecological systems, not quest hubs. Each place has residents, visitors, access rules, factions, secrets, resources, schedules and consequences.

## Current location network

- Old Port — ships, sailors, cargo, jobs, smuggling, guards.
- Market Square — merchants, finite goods, rumors, theft.
- Broken Lighthouse tavern — alcohol, gambling, romance, fights, gossip.
- Black Cliff Castle — king, queen, chancellor, court politics, wealth and restricted access.
- Guard Barracks — law, captain, arrests, future jail/bribery.
- Old Graveyard — undertaker, night activity, occult clues.
- Underground Crypt — hidden vampire presence, occult loot and future undead ecology.
- Ashen Moon Order lodge — initiation, rituals, forbidden magic and political secrecy.
- Temple of Dawn — priesthood, healing, anti-occult pressure and politics.
- Lower Streets — begging, homelessness, black market and smugglers.
- Star Circle Ruins — magical research and ancient secrets.
- Fisher Cove — fishing, ordinary work and sea life.

## Design rule

A location must change what can happen rather than merely changing the background. Example: the graveyard is ordinary by day; at night a skilled/curious player can discover the crypt. The vampire is hidden by day. Knowledge of the occult spreads through memory and relationships rather than map markers.

## Next expansion

1. Interiors as separate sub-scenes: castle hall, royal chambers, tavern interior, barracks/jail, crypt floors.
2. Access enforcement: doors, guards, keys, bribery, disguises, stealth.
3. Location economy: rent, beds, food stock, grave digging wages, temple donations, castle treasury.
4. Location events: royal feast, execution, funeral, occult ritual, vampire hunt, market fire, tavern brawl.
5. Faction conflict over locations: Crown vs council, Temple vs occult order, Guard vs underworld.
6. NPC migration: jobs, marriage, imprisonment, death and wealth can change where a person lives.
